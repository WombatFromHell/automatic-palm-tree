sopsctl init --from-ssh is the whole bridge: it needs both halves (<keypair> and <keypair>.pub,
checked before anything is converted), takes the public half through ssh-to-age -i for the
recipients and the private half through ssh-to-age -private-key, and asks for the passphrase
*before* anything is written, so a run that cannot get one leaves no temp file and no half-written
identity. ssh-to-age's own call runs </dev/null so it cannot reach a terminal behind the script's
back. A blank read is refused.

A rerun on the same identity is recognised by the `# created: ssh-to-age from ...` line; deriving
over an identity holding a different keypair is refused, because records sealed to the old
recipient would stop opening. That is a rotation (init without --from-ssh, then recipients and
rekey), not a rewrite. Nothing here copies, unlocks or rewrites the ssh files, and the keypair
stays 0600.
